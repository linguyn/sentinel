## Architecture Refactor

### The Original Goal and What Went Wrong

From the very beginning, Sentinel was intended to be an autonomous game-testing platform that could be integrated into different Unity games, not just one specific game.

However, as development progressed, I focused heavily on making the AI work inside my own Unity demo environment. I implemented movement, interaction, observation, and action tracking around the systems that already existed in that game.

Although these features worked, I gradually built an architecture that was too dependent on my demo game's implementation.

For example, Sentinel currently relies on components such as `CommonIntent`, `PlayerInteraction`, and manually configured observable entities. These work in my project, but another Unity developer might use completely different movement controllers, interaction systems, and gameplay mechanics.

I only recently realized that **the implementation was drifting away from the original goal**. Instead of building a reusable testing platform for Unity games, I was unintentionally building an AI testing agent specifically for my own game.

The problem wasn't that the existing features were useless, but that I hadn't properly separated Sentinel's core testing logic from game-specific implementations.

### The Refactor Decision

I decided to revisit the architecture to bring Sentinel back to its original purpose: **a reusable AI-assisted autonomous testing platform for Unity developers.**

Rather than requiring developers to adapt their games to Sentinel's existing demo-specific systems, Sentinel should be designed to understand and integrate with the systems that developers already use.

The new proposed workflow consists of five stages:

**1. UNDERSTAND**

AI analyzes relevant Unity scripts and scene objects to identify available gameplay actions, controls, and observable states.

**2. CONNECT**

AI generates or configures adapters that connect the game's existing systems to Sentinel's standard interfaces. The integration is then validated.

**3. PLAN**

AI determines what gameplay behaviors to test based on available mechanics, observable states, and expected outcomes.

**4. PLAY & CHECK**

Sentinel autonomously executes gameplay actions, observes state changes, and checks whether the results match expectations.

**5. REPORT**

Sentinel generates QA reports containing test outcomes, potential bugs, supporting evidence, and steps to reproduce.

### What This Means for the Project

The goal is not to discard everything I have already built.

The existing Unity–FastAPI communication, LLM decision-making, ActionTracker, ActionHistory, and observation system can still serve as the foundation.

However, the architecture needs clearer boundaries between the reusable Sentinel engine and game-specific integrations.

The five-stage workflow is currently a proposed direction rather than a completed implementation.

My main takeaway is that **a working prototype does not necessarily mean the architecture supports the original product requirements**. I need to consider reusability and integration boundaries while developing features, rather than only checking whether they work in my own environment.
